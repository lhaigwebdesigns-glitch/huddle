# Clear writing

## Process
1. Identify the reader and what they must know or do after reading. Write for them, not for yourself.
2. Lead with the point. Put the conclusion or answer in the first sentence or two, then support it.
3. One idea per paragraph. Open each paragraph with its topic sentence.
4. Prefer short, concrete words and active voice ("We shipped the fix" not "The fix was shipped").
5. Cut ruthlessly: remove filler ("very", "really", "in order to", "it is important to note that"), hedges, and repetition.
6. Use structure when it helps scanning: headings for long pieces, numbered lists for sequences, bullets for parallel items, tables for comparisons.
7. Define jargon on first use, or replace it.

## Self-check before sending
- Could a reader skim only the first line of each section and get the gist?
- Is every sentence earning its place?
- Are numbers, names and dates specific instead of vague ("soon", "many")?
- Does the tone match the context (casual chat vs. formal report)?
